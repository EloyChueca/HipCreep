## 1. Creep of conventional and cross-linked UHMWPE (dose and thermal treatment)

The radiation dose alone does not order the creep: the **thermal treatment after irradiation** (remelting above Tm vs
annealing below it) and the resulting crystallinity do.

### Laboratory

| Study | Material(s) | Finding on creep |
|---|---|---|
| Lewis & Carroll 2001, Biomed Mater Eng 11:167 | GUR 1050: control vs γ-crosslinked in air (low / high dose) | crosslinked creep compliance ×1.12–1.14 of control (compression) — used in HipCreep |
| Burger 2006 (PhD Pretoria); Burger & Weber 2004 (Orthop Proc) | γ 25 kGy air / N₂, annealed; 125 kGy (acetylene) | 25 kGy: 0.76–0.82 of virgin (2 h, RT); 125 kGy: 0.73 (RT), 0.55–0.63 at 50–60 °C — used |

## 2. Novelty check (points 1 and 2 of the method)

**Point 1 — minimum creep from the elevations (material above the original sphere, volume conservation).**
Not found. Closest work:
* Hernigou et al. 2008, Open Orthop J 2:51 (full text): flat unicompartmental tibial components; creep = CMM volume
  change − gravimetric wear (needs the original weight; not applicable to modular liners of unknown weight).
* Muratoglu et al. 2003, CORR 410:155: knee simulator, CMM scar volume vs gravimetric loss.
* Teeter et al. 2010 (J Arthroplasty 25:330; J Long Term Eff Med Implants 20:49): micro-CT deviation maps measure
  "wear plus creep" together; no separation.
* McDonald & Bloebaum 1995 (polarised light), Pezzotti 2011 / Miura 2015 / Tone 2020 (Raman): creep identified from
  the material structure, not from the geometry.
* Knee retrieval studies score "cold flow" qualitatively (Wasielewski 1994, Engh 1992, Cuckler 2003…).

**Point 2 — creep model + measured penetration + uncertainty, liner by liner.**
Not found. Separation has been done with: RSA time series (Sychterz 1999, Glyn-Jones 2008), Raman (Pezzotti, Miura,
Tone), gravimetry (Hernigou 2008), generic finite-element simulations (Bevill 2005, Penmetsa 2006, Liu 2012) or
laboratory creep tests (Estok 2005). None combines a creep model with recovery and Monte Carlo uncertainty with the 3D
penetration of each retrieved liner.

Caveat: Google Scholar and conference proceedings were only partly reachable.

## 3. Ways of estimating liner creep (search of 29 Oct 2026)

Already used or checked in HipCreep: Lee & Pienkowski 1998 (static law, recovery), Lee, Pienkowski & Lee 2003
(dynamic law), Deng 1998, Lewis & Carroll 2001, Galetz & Glatzel 2010, Burger 2006, Metin & Cengil 2016, Zeman 2018,
Takahashi 2016, Estok 2005, Hamai 2016, Rochcongar, Hernigou 2008, Teeter 2010 (see sections 1–2 and VALIDATION.md).

### Laboratory / simulator

| Study | What | Result useful for us |
|---|---|---|
| Lee & Pienkowski 1998, ASTM STP 1190-S | creep 10⁴ min static and 1 Hz at 2, 4, 8 MPa, then recovery 10⁴ min | recovery independent of load and load type: ≈ 51 % of the creep, 73 % of it within 1000 min (already the L&P scenario) |

## 4. Ideas for methods of our own (not found in the literature searched)

1. **Spatial-signature decomposition of the HipWear map.** Creep follows the contact pressure field (Winkler,
   centred on the load axis, volume-conserving: displaced material appears as elevations), wear follows pressure ×
   sliding distance (Archard; elongated along the gait paths, removes volume). Fit the measured deviation map
   δ(θ, φ) = a·S_creep + b·S_wear + c·S_bulge by least squares (weighted with the HipWear noise), with S computed
   analytically from the load axis, head/cup radii and a standard gait (ISO 14242 kinematics). The direction
   difference reported by Glyn-Jones 2008 is the one-dimensional version of this.
2. **In vivo creep law with saturation calibrated on clinical data.** `C(t) = C∞·[1 − exp(−(t/τ)^β)]`, with C∞
   from the HipCreep contact model (pressure, thickness, material) and τ, β fitted to the RSA time courses
   (≈ 95 % within 6 months). It replaces the open-ended log extrapolation of the 10⁴-minute laws for liners with
   years in vivo, and it is anchored on in vivo penetration rather than on uniaxial tests.
3. **Cohort calibration with censored bounds.** For each liner the remaining creep is bounded below by the
   elevations and above by D (or by the detection limit when D is not detected). A hierarchical (Bayesian or
   maximum-likelihood, interval-censored) fit of a creep multiplier per material over the series uses the
   retrieval data themselves to correct the laboratory laws.
4. **Physical ageing in the long-term extrapolation** (Struik effective time `t_eff = t_e·ln(1 + t/t_e)`): a
   physically motivated alternative to the "cap" and "log" extrapolations of the Lee laws.
5. **Backside / screw-hole extrusion as an in vivo creep gauge** (needs backside scans): the height of the
   material extruded into the screw holes, at a known hole diameter and an estimated backside pressure, gives the
   creep strain of that liner.
6. **Accelerated recovery test** (laboratory, not a model): rescan a retrieved liner after holding it at a moderate
   temperature (below ≈ 80 °C, to avoid changing crystallinity); with the Arrhenius shift the change measures the
   still-recoverable creep and checks the L&P vs long-loading scenarios. Raman residual strain (Miura 2015) is the
   established alternative.
